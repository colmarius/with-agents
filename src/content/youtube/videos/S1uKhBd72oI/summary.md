*All English wording below is an editorial translation/paraphrase of the Italian captions; nothing is a verbatim quotation.*

Salvatore Sanfilippo reads an Italian translation of his year-end 2025 article on AI. He argues that the field's rapid changes have weakened earlier dismissals of language-model scaling and reasoning, then offers personal forecasts about reinforcement learning, coding adoption, model architecture, AGI, and risk.

## Key Ideas

- Sanfilippo frames the piece as personal reflection in a field where specialists have also made consequential forecasting errors. His examples are academic skepticism about scaling autoregressive language models and the continued description of LLMs as stochastic parrots while their capabilities increased. [00:00:53]-[00:04:11]
- He says he wrote the English article without an LLM because he prefers imperfect personal wording to polished generated prose. The Italian version read in the video is itself AI-translated, which he warns may compound awkward phrasing. [00:04:11]-[00:05:32]
- His headline assessment is that, during 2025, the claim that LLMs contain no representation of prompt meaning or intended output largely receded. He presents chain of thought as a central way to improve model output. [00:05:32]-[00:06:41]
- He proposes two roles for chain of thought: bringing relevant concepts into context as a form of internal search, and, when combined with reinforcement learning, teaching token-by-token trajectories toward useful answers. [00:06:41]
- Verifiable rewards could extend improvement beyond a fixed stock of training text, he argues, because tasks such as optimizing program speed provide a measurable signal over repeated attempts. He predicts reinforcement learning will be a major source of further model progress. [00:06:41]-[00:07:46]
- He sees resistance to AI-assisted programming declining as its return on effort improves, while distinguishing conversational use of models as colleagues from delegation to autonomous coding agents. [00:07:46]-[00:08:54]
- Although some researchers are pursuing alternatives with explicit symbolic representations or world models, Sanfilippo thinks differentiable systems can approximate discrete reasoning and might reach AGI without a fundamentally new paradigm. He also expects radically different architectures could independently support similar capabilities. [00:08:54]-[00:10:09]
- He rejects the idea that chain of thought transformed LLMs into a fundamentally different kind of system: the same next-token architecture generates it token by token. He cites progress on ARC-AGI tasks as evidence against earlier claims that Transformers could not perform this kind of abstraction. [00:10:09]-[00:11:15]

## Practical Implications

- Separate observations about current model behavior from forecasts about reinforcement learning, AGI, and risk; each needs a different standard of evidence.
- For reinforcement-learning workloads, define rewards that are genuinely verifiable and resistant to shortcuts before treating additional training as useful progress.
- When evaluating reasoning improvements, distinguish base-model capacity, post-training, chain-of-thought prompting, and added inference-time compute.
- Compare assisted and agentic programming by task outcomes, review cost, and error recovery rather than treating all AI coding workflows as one category.

## Questions and Tensions

- The video narrates an opinion article rather than presenting a systematic review, and its historical generalizations and benchmark claims are not independently checked here.
- Observable gains from chain of thought do not by themselves establish that the generated text faithfully exposes the model's internal process.
- Verifiable rewards are clearest for domains with executable or numerical feedback; the argument does not show how well the approach transfers to objectives that are ambiguous or easy to game.
- The claims that current architectures may reach AGI and that extinction is the next decades' central challenge are forecasts, not conclusions established by the examples in the video.
