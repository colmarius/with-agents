## Key Ideas

- Kothari says education should retain discipline, social learning, and learning-how-to-learn while curricula adapt to computers, LLMs, and agents. He credits a Purdue co-op program with joining theory to practical work ([00:08:06]-[00:16:36]).

- Kothari says he invested in Notion because he knew founder Ivan Zhao and shared the vision of democratizing software creation. - Kothari argues that copying the current Notion with faster coding agents would miss a larger shift in knowledge work. His hypothesis is that a future productivity suite may center on conversations with agents rather than a conventional set of applications ([00:37:29]-[00:40:29]).
- Kothari describes Notion's strategy as starting with innovative small teams and growing with them into larger deployments. His claims about deployments, governance, and enterprise suitability are first-party company claims ([00:42:14]-[00:45:05]).

- Kothari positions Notion as multi-model infrastructure for human-agent collaboration and argues that companies building their own agent infrastructure must continually fund harness and context updates. ## Practical Implications

- Pair technical study with applied work and update curricula without discarding durable systems fundamentals.
- Start company formation from a customer problem and fast feedback, not fundraising or technology novelty alone.
- Reconsider the workflow before using agents merely to reproduce an existing product more quickly.
- Require access controls, data definitions, review, and outcome measures for agents operating over internal email, analytics, or sales data.
