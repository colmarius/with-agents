Theo Browne, identified in the source title as `@t3dotgg`, argues that newer coding models have progressed from reliable tool calls to longer-running work and then orchestration. He says developers will miss that shift if they keep assigning old-sized tasks and preserving workflows or identities built around earlier constraints ([00:00:54]-[00:03:29], [00:06:08]-[00:09:29]).

Browne uses project tiers and product breadth to make the argument concrete: some former services can now be scheduled Markdown instructions, while agent-assisted development may let small teams attempt wider platforms whose users extend missing vertical features themselves. He presents that widened scope as a direction to test, not a demonstrated claim that reliability or operational depth has become cheap ([00:09:29]-[00:15:40]).

## Key Ideas

- Browne describes Sonnet 3.5 as a reliable tool-call era, Opus 4.5 as a jump in longer-running task completion, and Mythos as a shift toward models decomposing, delegating, and verifying work through additional models ([00:00:54]-[00:03:29]).
- He compares current developer interfaces to skeuomorphic design: familiar terminals, tools, languages, and workflows can preserve earlier constraints even after their original rationale weakens ([00:04:21]-[00:07:46]).
- Browne challenges attachment to language identity, inherited Git workflows, and sunk code cost, arguing that developers should distinguish practices that remain useful from practices retained mainly through habit ([00:06:58]-[00:09:29]).
- He says stronger models lower his old project tiers: a former startup-sized idea can become a side project, while a recurring PR-triage service can become a Markdown instruction run on a schedule ([00:09:29]-[00:12:09]).
- Browne reframes “bigger” as wider product breadth. He argues that small teams can cover more verticals than before if their systems also let users implement specialized features the core product does not supply ([00:13:06]-[00:15:40]).

## Practical Implications

- Evaluate stronger agents with broader end-to-end assignments that require decomposition and verification, rather than assuming gains will appear on the same narrow prompts used for earlier models ([00:01:48]-[00:03:29]).
- Revisit inherited development practices one at a time and preserve them when they still enforce a real security, collaboration, or quality constraint—not only because the toolchain made them conventional ([00:06:08]-[00:09:29]).
- For agent-extensible products, separate broad initial coverage from deep reliability and expose extension points so users can add unsupported vertical behavior without requiring the core team to predict every use case ([00:13:06]-[00:15:40]).

## Questions and Tensions

- Browne's model-era progression is based on his experience and audience response; the talk does not provide a controlled evaluation of orchestration quality, cost, or failure rates ([00:00:54]-[00:03:29]).
- Lower implementation cost does not by itself establish that startup-scale reliability, security, support, or operations have fallen by the same tier; Browne explicitly distinguishes adding a database platform quickly from matching RDS reliability ([00:10:29]-[00:14:51]).
- Browne says he does not yet know what “too big” means, so the proposed move toward wider products remains an exploration of new limits rather than a bounded project-selection method ([00:12:09]-[00:13:06]).
