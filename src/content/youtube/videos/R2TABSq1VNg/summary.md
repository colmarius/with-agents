Salvatore Sanfilippo argues that AGI is too imprecise to support a single useful threshold. He proposes separating it into industrial, physiological, and epistemological forms so that economic substitution, embodied competence, and the creation of genuinely new knowledge can be discussed as distinct achievements.

## Key Ideas

- AGI has no strict scientific definition, so the term can be stretched either to minimize AI progress or to portray full general intelligence as imminent. [00:00:00] Sanfilippo treats that ambiguity as a reason to use more functional categories. [00:05:42]-[00:07:00]
- In the conventional ladder, narrow intelligence performs a specific task, AGI can perform any intellectual task available to a person, and artificial superintelligence exceeds human intellectual frontiers. Under a broad reading, AGI would therefore include scientific invention. [00:00:58]-[00:03:01]
- Superintelligence could arise by running many human-equivalent synthetic researchers much faster or in parallel, by scaling one system to greater individual abstraction and reasoning, or by combining both routes. [00:04:00]-[00:05:42]
- Industrial AGI would be general across knowledge-work domains and able to replace most human participation in those workflows. Sanfilippo sees coding and other agents as partial precursors because they already cross task boundaries but still require supervision. [00:07:00]-[00:08:10]
- Physiological AGI would add mammal-like control of a body: navigation, object manipulation, and physical work. Combined with industrial competence, it could perform roles that require both domain knowledge and embodied action. [00:09:01]
- He attributes much LLM progress to learning from existing human-produced data. Epistemological AGI would require architectures or learning systems able to reorganize that base into scientific knowledge that did not previously exist. [00:10:11]
- Sanfilippo distinguishes exploring overlooked combinations inside the existing knowledge space from making a conceptual leap that changes the space itself. He considers the latter the hardest and qualitatively different form of AGI. [00:11:23]-[00:12:30]

## Practical Implications

- Specify whether an AGI claim concerns autonomous knowledge work, embodied work, or open-ended discovery instead of treating them as one milestone.
- Evaluate industrial systems by how much of a complete workflow they can perform without supervision, not only by the range of isolated tasks they can attempt.
- Keep throughput from parallel or accelerated systems separate from evidence that one system has greater reasoning ability.
- Design scientific-discovery evaluations that distinguish recombination of known material from the production and validation of new explanatory ideas.

## Questions and Tensions

- The three-part scheme is a proposed conceptual framework, not an experimentally validated measurement system.
- The boundary between sophisticated recombination and a genuinely new conceptual category may be difficult to observe or test.
- The suggested path from supervised agents to industrial substitution does not establish a timeline or account for reliability, governance, and organizational constraints.
- Embodied competence and scientific invention may depend on capabilities that do not divide cleanly from language-based knowledge work.
