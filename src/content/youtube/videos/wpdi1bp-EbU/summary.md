REWORK host Kimberly Rhodes asks 37signals co-founders Jason Fried and David Heinemeier Hansson whether agent-built custom software will end SaaS. The coding-agent discussion runs from approximately [00:05:12] to [00:18:29]; the episode's other listener questions concern competition and personal communication.

## Key Ideas

- Hansson says replacing existing SaaS with agents remains pioneer behavior driven largely by people who already want to experiment, with greater near-term exposure among expensive and disliked enterprise systems. [00:05:12]-[00:07:55]
- Buying software transfers operational responsibility: customers avoid running services, maintaining them, managing data and backups, and preserving ownership when employees leave. [00:07:55]-[00:09:42]
- Fried frames agent-built software as another competitive option rather than a wholly new source of churn and recommends evaluating the actual customer segment and price point. [00:09:42]-[00:10:44]
- Fried argues that employee time, maintenance, breakage, and opportunity cost can outweigh replacing an inexpensive subscription, even when agents reduce implementation effort. [00:10:44]-[00:12:47]
- Fried says implementation is only part of a product; cohesive concepts, boundaries, workflow understanding, and operational support remain necessary. [00:12:47]-[00:14:30]
- Hansson distinguishes implementing a customer's request from diagnosing the problem and deciding what software should exist, saying his strongest examples still require human taste, vision, and domain knowledge. [00:14:30]-[00:16:38]
- Hansson expects a nearer-term competitive effect from people who already have product ideas and taste but previously lacked the implementation skills or funding to build them. [00:16:38]-[00:18:29]

## Practical Implications

- Evaluate build-versus-buy decisions across the full ownership lifecycle, not implementation cost alone.
- Segment agent-driven competitive risk by customer incentives, price, customization, and operational appetite.
- Treat faster implementation as an expansion of human product capacity rather than evidence of autonomous product direction.

## Questions and Tensions

- The market and adoption claims are first-party observations and forecasts rather than measured industry evidence.
- 37signals' ONCE uptake provides experience from one vendor, not a general test of customer willingness to self-host agent-built software.
- Agent capability, implementation cost, and adoption are time-sensitive, so the August 2026 boundary should not be treated as permanent.
