Gergely Orosz, host of The Pragmatic Engineer, interviews software engineer Grady Booch, identified in the title and introduction as a co-creator of UML and a pioneer of object-oriented design. Booch uses computing history to argue that AI coding agents are another rise in abstraction, while software engineering remains the work of balancing technical, economic, human, ethical, safety, and security forces ([00:00:00]-[00:04:57], [00:46:46]-[00:49:40]).

## Key Ideas

- Booch defines software engineering as building reasonably optimal rather than perfect systems under competing static and dynamic forces. Code is one mechanism inside that broader systems trade-off, not the profession's complete output ([00:01:59]-[00:04:57]).
- He organizes software history around recurring abstraction shifts: early algorithmic and data-oriented work, object-oriented classes and objects, then libraries, packages, services, and platforms. Each shift made prior details less central while enabling larger systems ([00:08:54]-[00:10:59], [00:32:38]-[00:38:41], [00:46:46]-[00:48:44]).
- In this framing, agents such as Cursor and ChatGPT did not begin the third golden age. They help developers navigate and apply the libraries and system components that already raised software's abstraction level around the turn of the millennium ([00:46:46]-[00:48:44]).
- Booch distinguishes disposable personal software from software intended to endure. He welcomes agent-built, single-user automation where failure and maintenance costs are limited, but says durable systems still require fundamentals and engineering judgment ([00:53:50]-[00:56:46]).
- He argues that agents are strongest on recurring patterns represented many times in training data, such as familiar web and CRUD structures. That makes pattern generation useful without establishing that agents cover the full range of computing systems ([01:00:35]-[01:02:45]).
- His D3 example presents agents as guided learning tools: he asks Cursor for a minimal example, studies the result, and iterates in conversation. He qualifies the success by saying the domain was well structured and repeatedly implemented before ([01:03:41]-[01:05:33]).
- Booch expects some app-development and delivery-pipeline work to be automated, but advises developers to move toward systems-level skills: managing complexity, architecture, scale, and interacting human and technical forces ([01:06:26]-[01:09:05]).
- For multi-agent systems, he points to established coordination patterns rather than treating orchestration as unprecedented: Minsky's *Society of Mind*, blackboards and global workspaces, and Brooks's subsumption architectures ([01:09:05]-[01:13:10]).

## Practical Implications

- Decide whether software is disposable or durable before choosing review, testing, security, documentation, and maintenance rigor ([00:54:55]-[00:56:46]).
- Use agents to shorten the path into an unfamiliar but established library, while retaining enough foundation to inspect and correct what they produce ([00:57:49]-[00:58:38], [01:03:41]-[01:05:33]).
- Invest beyond code generation in systems theory, architecture, safety, security, economics, ethics, and coordination across people and agents ([00:48:44]-[00:49:40], [01:08:01]-[01:13:10]).
- Reuse known coordination structures when designing agent systems, then validate whether they fit the actual workload rather than assuming novelty requires a novel architecture ([01:11:10]-[01:13:10]).

## Questions and Tensions

- The three-golden-ages model is Booch's historical synthesis; the episode does not define criteria that would let readers test its boundaries or compare it with another periodization ([00:04:57]-[00:08:54], [00:46:46]-[00:48:44]).
- Booch's analogy to compilers supports continuity, but does not quantify the pace, breadth, or labor impact of the current abstraction shift. He does expect losses in some delivery and simple-app roles ([00:51:45]-[00:54:55], [01:06:26]-[01:08:01]).
- His claim that today's agents mostly automate recurring patterns is plausible within his examples, but the interview supplies no task suite, model comparison, or boundary test across less familiar domains ([01:00:35]-[01:05:33]).
- The distinction between disposable and enduring software is useful but incomplete: a personal tool can acquire users, sensitive data, or operational dependencies after its initial creation ([00:54:55]-[00:56:46]).
- The established systems and biological patterns are directions for agent architecture, not evaluated designs in this interview; reliability, observability, conflict resolution, and governance remain open ([01:09:05]-[01:13:10]).
