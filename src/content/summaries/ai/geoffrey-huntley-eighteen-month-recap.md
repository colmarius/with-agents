Geoffrey Huntley argues that cheaper software implementation changes both who can build products and how companies should organize the work. His practical advice is to practice deliberately, understand an agent's mechanics, and remove process waste. His stronger claims about labor costs and small teams displacing incumbents are hypotheses and anecdotes, not demonstrated industry-wide outcomes.

The [article](https://ghuntley.com/eighteen-month-recap/), published September 27, presents an edited transcript of his May 2026 Singapore talk. This summary checks it against the embedded conference recording's English auto-generated captions; obvious caption errors were corrected using the article, without original-audio verification. The saved transcript covers the linked segment, approximately [1:53:33–2:10:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m12vGjfbNlo&t=6813s), with timestamps on the full recording's timeline.

### Practice and technical understanding

- **Broader access is not proof of cheaper end-to-end delivery.** Huntley describes designers, product managers, and a tour guide using AI to build software, comparing the change to smartphones broadening photography. These examples illustrate access to implementation. The talk does not establish total delivery cost, including verification, operation, and maintenance, so its “less than minimum wage” headline should not be read as a measured engineering-cost comparison [1:53:33–1:57:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m12vGjfbNlo&t=6813s).
- **Treat agent use as a practiced skill, not a one-off product test.** His musical-instrument analogy argues for repeated experimentation rather than rejecting a tool after an unsuccessful attempt. He also hypothesizes that adoption jumps during holidays because people finally have time to discover improved capabilities; that timing explanation is not a measured adoption result [1:58:20–2:00:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m12vGjfbNlo&t=7100s).
- **Learn the mechanism by building an agent.** Huntley's hiring criteria include explaining tool calls, the sequence of model inference, model-card differences, and temperature. He recommends his roughly 300-line [coding-agent workshop](https://ghuntley.com/agent/) as a way to move from using a product to understanding its internals. This is an educational exercise and his hiring preference, not proof that a short agent implements an entire commercial coding product [2:06:57–2:09:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m12vGjfbNlo&t=7617s), [2:09:54–2:10:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m12vGjfbNlo&t=7794s).

### Organizational change and its limits

- **Redesign the work, not just the model selection.** Huntley contrasts startups that adapt their codebases and processes around agents with incumbents that must change established habits and structures. He predicts small teams will compete with fewer people while larger organizations spend years adapting. The contrast is his competitive-disruption thesis, not a controlled comparison of company performance [2:00:21–2:02:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m12vGjfbNlo&t=7221s).
- **Cheaper construction changes the capital question, but does not eliminate it.** He asks whether small teams still need seed funding primarily to hire people to build a product. He explicitly says software remains investable and leaves nuances unexplored in the talk. The edited article adds a recommendation to redirect capital toward human go-to-market expansion; that specific prescription is in the written version, not the corresponding caption passage [2:02:18–2:03:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m12vGjfbNlo&t=7338s), [edited article](https://ghuntley.com/eighteen-month-recap/).
- **Staffing anecdotes do not establish a general productivity ratio.** He relays a founder's account of shrinking from roughly 60 to 20 people by not replacing departures while increasing output. The edited article quotes “30x” output, but neither version supplies a measurement method or independent evidence. Huntley also says he is not advocating wholesale organizational upheaval and acknowledges uncertainty and distress for workers [2:05:09–2:06:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m12vGjfbNlo&t=7509s), [edited article](https://ghuntley.com/eighteen-month-recap/).
- **Ask managers what they have changed.** His concrete interview question is how a manager has revised systems and processes in response to agents. He argues that removing organizational waste can accelerate work more than AI itself. This recommendation can be considered separately from his categorical advice to leave employers that ban AI; **editorial qualification:** that advice does not account for security, contractual, or regulatory constraints [2:09:00–2:09:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m12vGjfbNlo&t=7740s), [2:06:04–2:06:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m12vGjfbNlo&t=7564s).
