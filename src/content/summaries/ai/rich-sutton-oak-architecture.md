Rich Sutton presents OaK, a proposed reinforcement-learning agent design in which all learning happens while the agent acts in the world. The agent turns its own state features into subproblems, learns ways of behaving that solve them, models what those behaviors lead to, and plans with those models. Sutton presents it as a research vision: he marks several components as conceptually understood but not yet workable with deep learning, and one as lacking a specific proposal.

This summary is based on the English auto-generated captions, with names and technical terms corrected where the captions were clearly wrong. The talk was given at the Reinforcement Learning Conference (RLC) 2025. Sutton says it is a new talk, finished the night before, and not yet polished. His statements that AI is "an unalloyed good" and that the path to strong AI runs through reinforcement learning rather than large language models are his stated positions, not arguments developed in the talk.

## Key points

- **OaK stands for options and knowledge.** An option is a way of behaving paired with a rule for when to stop; Sutton drops the third element, the initiation set, that the standard definition includes. The agent's knowledge is a model of what happens when it follows each option. Because these models describe extended behavior rather than single actions, the agent can plan in larger steps. [03:02–04:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=182s)
- **The design has three goals.** It should be domain general, with nothing specific to any particular world built in. It should be experiential, so the mind grows from experience while running rather than from a separate training phase. Its abstractions should be open-ended, limited only by computing resources. Sutton contrasts this with large language models, which do their learning at design time and do not learn when deployed. [04:06–06:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=246s), [06:16–07:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=376s)
- **Excluding domain knowledge serves understanding, not immediate performance.** Sutton says building in knowledge of the world is right if you want an application that performs well immediately. His goal is different: a conceptually simple account of how a mind works. He quotes his Bitter Lesson essay, which argues for building in only the methods that find and capture the world's complexity, so agents discover things as we do. [07:19–10:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=439s), [10:50–11:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=650s)
- **A big world forces learning at runtime.** The "big world" hypothesis says the world is far larger than the agent, partly because it contains other agents whose minds matter. The agent's value functions, policies, and models must therefore be approximations, and the world appears to change over time. An AI agent starting a job, for example, must learn its coworkers' names and the state of their project. Since the agent must be able to learn, form abstractions, model, and plan at runtime anyway, Sutton treats doing these at design time as an optional head start that complicates the design. [11:46–13:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=706s), [13:40–17:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=820s)
- **Runtime learning scales with compute, but current methods fall short.** Sutton argues that runtime learning adapts to the part of the world actually encountered and scales with available computation. Design-time work scales with available human expertise. Runtime learning also enables meta-learning, which means comparing ways of learning over time and keeping the better one. He concedes that today's continual deep-learning methods "don't work very well". [19:40–22:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=1180s)
- **Goals are expressed as a single scalar reward.** Sutton defends the reward hypothesis: all goals can be treated as maximizing the expected cumulative sum of one reward signal. He cites the paper "Settling the Reward Hypothesis" by Michael Bowling and others as showing that, in a certain sense, constraints, multiple objectives, and risk sensitivity do not add generality. He also cites the "Reward is Enough" paper and admits he prefers simplicity, possibly "a little bit too far". [22:10–25:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=1330s)
- **OaK extends model-based reinforcement learning with subproblems.** The base architecture already includes a policy, a value function, a learned model, a planner, and a perception process. Perception builds a sense of the current state from past actions and observations, because the agent receives observations, not the world's true state. OaK adds many auxiliary subproblems. Each has its own policy and value function and is based on one component of the state feature vector. [25:10–29:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=1510s)
- **The architecture runs eight processes in parallel.** The agent learns a policy and value function for reward, generates new features, ranks them, creates subproblems for the highest-ranked features, and learns options that solve them. It then learns models of those options, plans with the models, and tracks how useful each element is, discarding some and proposing new ones. Sutton marks learning for the main task as conceptually done but waiting on reliable continual deep learning. He marks feature generation as having many ideas but no specific proposal. [29:20–31:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=1760s), [31:14–33:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=1874s), [33:26–34:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=2006s)
- **The agent must create its own subproblems.** Sutton argues that suitable subproblems vary too much between worlds to be supplied at design time. He uses a young orangutan swinging, an orca balancing a buoy, and an infant moving between toys as illustrations of play: setting oneself problems to learn about and control. These are analogies for the intended behavior, not evidence for the mechanism. [34:28–36:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=2068s), [36:24–38:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=2184s)
- **Each subproblem is to reach a feature without sacrificing too much reward.** Sutton calls these "reward-respecting subproblems of feature attainment". Given a feature and a weight for how strongly it is wanted, the agent seeks an option that maximizes three things together: the reward received while following the option, the weighted value of the feature when the option stops, and the value of the state where it stops. The last term rules out reaching a goal by entering a bad state. His examples are getting coffee in a way that gets you arrested, or walking along a cliff edge and falling off. [38:31–41:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=2311s), [41:13–43:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=2473s)
- **Usefulness feeds back to decide which features matter.** Options, option models, and planning all use state features. When a model or option proves useful, that judgment flows back to the subproblem and feature behind it, and this feedback ranks the features. The result is a cycle: features define subproblems, their solutions become options, option models support planning, and feedback on usefulness reranks the features. Sutton says standard off-policy algorithms for general value functions (GVFs), such as GTD, emphatic TD, Retrace, and ABQ, can learn most of these predictions. [43:09–46:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=2589s), [46:19–48:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=2779s)
- **Planning keeps values consistent with a model when wants change.** Sutton argues that the world's dynamics often stay the same while the value of states changes. A restroom is always reachable, but you do not always want to go there. It is often easier to learn the model correctly and plan than to relearn values directly. Planning with option models uses the same value-iteration update as planning with single actions: replace each action with an option, and use the reward and next-state distribution for following that option. With function approximation, values and models become parameterized, and he skips the computational-cost details. [47:22–50:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=2842s), [50:25–53:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=3025s)
- **Continual learning and feature generation remain open.** Reliable continual learning exists for linear and tabular methods but not yet for deep networks, which suffer catastrophic forgetting and loss of plasticity. Sutton describes candidate fixes, including continual backprop and generate-and-test methods that create features and test their usefulness, but says he has no specific proposal. He forecasts that the problem may be solved in the next couple of years and expects his older IDBD algorithm to play a key role. Both are predictions. [53:28–56:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=3208s), [56:12–57:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=3372s)
- **Sutton claims explanatory value for the whole design.** He argues that reinforcement learning and OaK offer the first plausible mechanistic answers to questions such as where concepts come from, what reasoning and play are for, and how perception can work without human labels. He calls OaK a computational theory of intelligence and a guide for research. He also calls its answers to where subproblems, options, and features come from "admittedly incomplete". [57:18–59:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=3438s), [59:21–1:01:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEbbGyNkR2U&t=3561s)

## Limits

The talk presents a design and its rationale, not experiments or results. Its main dependencies, reliable continual deep learning and automatic feature generation, are unsolved by Sutton's own account. For a later interview in which Sutton and Khurram Javed discuss continual learning, the big world hypothesis, and Oak Lab's research plans, see [Why AI Models Stop Learning](/summaries/coding-with-agents/why-ai-models-stop-learning-rich-sutton-khurram-javed).
