Fireship examines developer frustration with GitHub reliability in April 2026 and reports Mitchell Hashimoto's decision to move Ghostty away from the platform. His explanation connects infrastructure incidents with the concentration of code hosting, collaboration, and automation in one service. This summary uses English auto-generated captions; incident details and uptime figures remain attributed reports.

## Key ideas

- **GitHub is more than remote Git storage.** Teams also depend on issues, pull requests, search, and Actions for coordination, testing, and deployment. An interruption can therefore block work even when developers still have local copies of their repositories. [00:47–02:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d53Zk28esmU&t=47s)
- **The video presents conflicting reliability measures.** Fireship cites low third-party uptime estimates while acknowledging that GitHub's own status page reports above 99% for its services. He does not reconcile the measurement methods, so the percentages should not be treated as directly comparable. He also reports merge-queue, search, and security incidents during the preceding week. [01:50–03:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d53Zk28esmU&t=110s)
- **Repeated disruption can outweigh a platform's network benefits.** Fireship describes Hashimoto keeping a journal of days when outages blocked him before announcing Ghostty's departure. This is an individual developer's reported experience, not a measurement of all users' reliability. [02:43–04:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d53Zk28esmU&t=163s)
- **Agent workloads are offered as part of the explanation.** Fireship cites GitHub leadership describing sharply increased agentic development activity, then interprets that as additional pressure on the platform. The video does not isolate agent traffic as the cause of each incident. It names GitLab, Codeberg, and SourceHut as alternatives without benchmarking them. [03:44–05:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d53Zk28esmU&t=224s)

## Practical implications

- Editorial: Distinguish repository availability from the availability of the surrounding development workflow. A local clone does not replace hosted issues, review state, search, or CI.

## Questions and limits

- Editorial: The video does not provide enough measurement detail to resolve its uptime comparison. Its S3 comparison and language about lost code should not substitute for service-specific incident reports or evidence of permanent repository loss.
