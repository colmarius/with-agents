Fireship presents Meta Connect 2026 as a pivot toward an always-available personal agent called Muse. This summary relies on English auto-generated captions, not inspection of keynote footage or hardware. Product specifications, privacy behavior, prices, and release dates are Fireship's dated account rather than independently verified current facts.

## Key ideas

- Muse is described as an assistant spanning web browsing, computer use, email, and wearable devices, with third-party connectors. Fireship says the free service takes a cut of purchases it makes, connecting its business model to transactions rather than a subscription. [00:00–02:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c1rPlzxSZ8E&t=0s)
- The described architecture gives each user a cloud Linux VM with a browser, storage, and an agent harness. Credentials remain in a separate process: the agent receives placeholder tokens, and a gatekeeper substitutes the real credentials when sending requests. The proposed protection limits what a prompt-injected agent can directly expose. [02:06–02:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c1rPlzxSZ8E&t=126s)
- Fireship distinguishes isolation from the agent from privacy against Meta itself. He says a version intended to hide VM contents from Meta is still in testing, while the default service can use interactions for model training; he therefore challenges the broader keynote privacy framing. These implementation and policy claims require official confirmation before reuse as guidance. [02:53–03:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c1rPlzxSZ8E&t=173s)
- The hardware roundup includes planned lightweight VR glasses, refreshed camera and audio glasses, and a connected Muse Charm device. The quoted prices and shipping windows are announcement-time claims, not evidence of actual availability or hands-on performance. [02:53–04:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c1rPlzxSZ8E&t=173s)

## Practical implications

Editorial: Evaluate credential isolation, provider access, and model-training use as separate questions. A system can keep passwords out of the model's context while still exposing other user activity to its operator.
