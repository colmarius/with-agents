Fireship surveys reported model launches from Anthropic, Meta, and OpenAI, questioning whether Astra's launch claims establish AGI. This summary uses English auto-generated captions only. Fireship says he lacked Astra access, so its capabilities here come from reported benchmarks and other people's demonstrations, not his own test or an independent visual inspection.

## Key ideas

- For Anthropic's Fable and Mythos 5.1, Fireship emphasizes reported case studies rather than scores: tracing a rare crash into a compiled vendor library, raising the success rate for designing proteins that bind a target from about 10% to about 50%, and mapping part of Venus from old NASA radar data. These are attributed case studies; the transcript does not establish their evaluation methods or general success rates. [01:02–02:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FluKUJyeYD8&t=62s)
- Meta's Muse Spark 1.3 has a contributor tier at 10 cents input and 20 cents output, against $1.25 and $4.25 for the standard endpoint, if users let Meta train on everything they send. Data-use terms are therefore part of the price comparison; the cheapest endpoint is not equivalent to the standard one. Quoted rates are dated announcement claims. [01:50–03:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FluKUJyeYD8&t=110s)
- Astra's reported launch involved a removed announcement, delayed public access, and initially restricted early access. Fireship distinguishes promised rollout from actual availability and treats the theory that Astra caused simultaneous competitor outages as a joke, not a supported explanation. [02:53–04:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FluKUJyeYD8&t=173s)
- Fireship presents computer use and spatial tasks as Astra's main pitch, citing a reported OSWorld desktop-task score of 73% at about 40 minutes per task, versus 65% at 75 minutes for GPT-5.6 Soul, along with Blender and Unreal demonstrations. He attributes to OpenAI the claim that Astra is the first model to reach the "critical" cyber threshold in its preparedness framework, meaning it can find and exploit zero-days without human direction; neither the benchmarks nor demos independently establish broad autonomous reliability. [03:56–07:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FluKUJyeYD8&t=236s)
- He contrasts favorable vendor benchmarks and early-access impressions with Artificial Analysis's independent index, which reportedly scored Astra 61, the same as GPT-5.6 Soul and 5 points behind Fable 5.1. The mismatch suggests that performance depends on the task and evaluation rather than resolving into a single AGI verdict. [04:57–07:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FluKUJyeYD8&t=297s)

## Practical implications

Editorial: Match evaluation to the intended workload and distinguish demonstrations, vendor tests, and independent comparisons. Check access, current pricing, and data-use terms before choosing between announced models.

## Questions and limits

Editorial: The report does not define a sufficient test for AGI or independently validate its numerical claims. CodeRabbit Security's closing vulnerability-detection features and free scans are sponsored promotion, not evidence for the model comparisons.
