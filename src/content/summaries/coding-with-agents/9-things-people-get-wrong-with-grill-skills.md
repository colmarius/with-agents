Matt Pocock explains why Grill Me and Grill with Docs depend on engineering judgment rather than replacing it. The useful workflow is not an unlimited interview: choose answerable questions, keep the scope bounded, steer actively, and preserve the decisions for implementation or a deliberate handoff.

### Key Points Covered

- **Match the method to the question's fidelity**: Conversational grilling can settle low-fidelity decisions, while questions about how an interface feels or behaves often need a prototype before the planning session can continue [01:01–03:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UzMNBN6xLLA&t=61s).
- **Bound the scope before the interview expands**: Large scopes hide high-fidelity questions, encourage speculative planning, and consume context before reaching a useful decision [03:08–05:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UzMNBN6xLLA&t=188s).
- **Treat grilling as a steered conversation**: Passive use can let question count and scope grow without control; staying in discussion too long can also delay the executable evidence needed to answer the question [05:08–07:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UzMNBN6xLLA&t=308s).
- **Preserve the decision history**: Continue directly into implementation when context permits, or create a PRD or handoff from the same session rather than clearing first and discarding the alignment [07:07–09:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UzMNBN6xLLA&t=427s).
- **Use model capability and concurrency deliberately**: Pocock prefers a stronger model for broad planning questions and usually alternates between two sessions, but these are personal workflow heuristics rather than measured thresholds or throughput results [09:02–11:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UzMNBN6xLLA&t=542s).
