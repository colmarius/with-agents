Merge co-founder and CTO Gil Feig joins host Mayank Gupta to discuss bounded agent autonomy, enterprise engineering controls, context infrastructure, multi-model routing, and direct customer research.

### Key Points Covered

- **Autonomy should follow product risk**: Feig says Merge gives agents tighter bounds on its mature enterprise product and more room on newer products with fewer customers and edge cases. His “100x engineer” and 100-worker framing is aspirational rather than measured; he reports personally managing roughly two or three concurrent tasks [00:06:58]-[00:09:27].
- **Enterprise delivery retains layered checks**: He describes approved company context, local skills, tests and linting, AI review, and final human review, arguing that customer obligations make unattended production changes unrealistic [00:10:21]-[00:12:56].
- **Better models may absorb orchestration work**: Feig assesses OpenClaw as a useful demonstration of local agents but says it was expensive and inaccurate; in his view, model improvements, with some orchestration, will resolve more of those limitations [00:14:40]-[00:16:21].
- **Context combines synchronized and live data**: He argues that synchronized RAG remains useful alongside live API access, then presents Merge's portable context layer as a shared route to governance, observability, and model routing. These capabilities are Merge's first-party product thesis [00:17:20]-[00:20:41].
- **Merge claims multi-model savings**: Feig says Merge Fusion sends prompts to multiple models and uses a judge to select or combine responses, claiming 20–60% savings and equal-or-better results for selected combinations. The interview does not independently verify the savings, evaluations, or scale [00:22:39]-[00:24:21].
- **Coding has moved up an abstraction layer**: Responding to whether coding is solved, Feig compares natural-language programming with earlier transitions from assembly to higher-level languages and says architecture and product judgment still require human input [00:41:59]-[00:43:37].
- **Customer conversations beat feature research alone**: Feig recommends speaking directly with prospective users and reports that Merge conducted roughly 100 interviews before launch to uncover objections that product comparisons and AI research would miss [00:46:12]-[00:49:46].
