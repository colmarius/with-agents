Running agents in production requires more than checking one generated change: someone must own state, retries, recovery, and the consequences of failure. This editorial selection of six talks from AI Engineer's Agent Reliability playlist approaches those decisions from different angles, from when automation is worth its cost to whether it improves human control.

### Where to start

1. **Is this agent worth running when failures and supervision have real costs?** [Harrison Chase](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kTnfJszFxCg&t=118s) of LangChain/LangGraph frames the choice through success probability, successful-outcome value, failure cost, and operating cost ([01:58–02:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kTnfJszFxCg&t=118s)). Reversible actions and human approval can bound consequential failures, while background execution may increase useful concurrency without implying full autonomy ([09:48–17:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kTnfJszFxCg&t=588s)). The equation is a decision frame, not a supplied measurement method; correlated failures and review capacity still need local evidence.
2. **Which decisions belong to models, and which belong to deterministic control?** [Sam Bhagwat](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8SUJEqQNClw&t=436s) of Mastra.ai treats agents and workflows as composable primitives: agents provide iterative flexibility, while workflows add branching, parallelism, suspension, resumption, and replay ([07:16–09:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8SUJEqQNClw&t=436s)). He recommends adding narrower calls or explicit structure where broad model behavior becomes unreliable ([09:21–11:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8SUJEqQNClw&t=561s)). The talk provides architecture guidance, not comparative reliability measurements.
3. **Who owns long-running state, retries, and recovery?** [Preeti Somal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1izYWsokr9s&t=55s) of Temporal presents agentic applications as distributed systems spanning unreliable calls, parallel work, human interaction, and long-lived state ([00:55–03:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1izYWsokr9s&t=55s)). Her example separates code-defined business workflows from platform-managed execution state, retries, signals, queries, and history ([09:54–13:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1izYWsokr9s&t=594s)). This is a vendor presentation, and the design introduces a platform and programming-model dependency.
4. **How can plans and tools remain bounded and interpretable?** [Yogendra Miraje](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sl3icG-IjHo&t=318s) of FactSet separates blueprint generation, planning, execution, joining, and bounded replanning ([05:18–06:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sl3icG-IjHo&t=318s)). Natural-language blueprints restrict tool context and reduce planner load, while capability-oriented contracts, validation brakes, and component-level evaluation make failures easier to locate ([06:18–11:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sl3icG-IjHo&t=378s)). The blueprint is interpretable, but the talk does not establish who approves changes before execution.
5. **How should reliability span the software lifecycle?** [Itamar Friedman](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n991Yxo1aOI&t=323s), CEO and co-founder of Qodo, connects context, generation, testing, review, organizational practices, and explicit success criteria into specialist stages ([05:23–15:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n991Yxo1aOI&t=323s)). He demonstrates sequential CLI composition and distinguishes it from future-facing A2A coordination ([16:42–20:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n991Yxo1aOI&t=1002s)). Much of the evidence comes from Qodo's product architecture, and Friedman reports that A2A adoption among practitioners he asked was effectively absent.
6. **Does the system increase human agency, or only agent autonomy?** [Danielle Perszyk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dj0b_cEBHBI&t=256s), a cognitive scientist at Amazon AGI, contrasts autonomous-system goals with tools that increase human leverage and warns that automation can reduce agency ([04:16–07:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dj0b_cEBHBI&t=256s)). She connects controllable atomic interactions and a shared environment to broader outcomes such as creativity, productivity, strategic thinking, and flow ([10:30–19:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dj0b_cEBHBI&t=630s)). Those outcomes are difficult to operationalize and raise unresolved questions about privacy, consent, representation, and metric gaming.

### Use the collection as an operating-design review

Editorially, the six talks suggest a compact sequence: justify the expected value, mark deterministic and model-controlled boundaries, assign durable state and recovery ownership, bound plans and tools, evaluate the composed lifecycle, and check whether the result improves human control. Component reliability does not automatically transfer to the system around it.
