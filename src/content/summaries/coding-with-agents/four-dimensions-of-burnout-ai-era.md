Dominika Rogala applies a general workplace-burnout model to engineering leadership: map four warning signs, audit job demands and resources, then reduce demands and build resources. Her narrower AI-era argument is that lower effort per task does not guarantee lower human load because people still supervise output while adapting to faster tools and rising expectations.

This is a practitioner talk, not a study of coding-agent teams. It does not measure burnout before and after agent adoption, compare interventions, or define workload and review thresholds. Several research claims are named only at the institution or conference level, so the framework is best treated as a discussion aid rather than a validated diagnostic or causal account of AI-related burnout.

### Framework and Team Actions

- **Three dimensions come from common burnout descriptions; the fourth is the speaker's addition**: Rogala maps exhaustion, emotional distance, and reduced efficacy, then adds an “identity gap” between the professional someone wants to become and how they experience themselves at work. The talk does not validate that added dimension as a diagnostic measure ([00:03:27–00:06:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iJGKH45w4Q8&t=207s)).
- **Audit demands and resources instead of treating burnout as an individual resilience failure**: Teams can list sustained costs such as workload, context switching, cognitive load, always-on communication, and adaptation alongside resources such as clarity, tools, support, meaning, and room to recover. Rogala cautions that resources do not simply cancel demands and that people need enough space to use them ([00:06:58–00:10:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iJGKH45w4Q8&t=418s), [00:21:06–00:21:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iJGKH45w4Q8&t=1266s)).
- **AI may move rather than remove human effort**: Rogala argues that AI reduces effort per task but leaves people accountable for agent output and adds cognitive load, context switching, and continual adaptation. She also says efficiency gains become higher expectations. The talk supplies no coding tasks, adoption cohort, review data, or pre/post outcomes that test those claims ([00:10:32–00:13:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iJGKH45w4Q8&t=632s)).
- **The team-level levers are concrete but not coding-agent-specific**: Suggested changes include fewer priorities and status meetings, realistic plans, local decision authority, two or three protected deep-work windows, and designated on-the-clock time to explore new tools. These are general work-design proposals; the talk does not show how a team selected, trialed, or measured them ([00:13:10–00:15:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iJGKH45w4Q8&t=790s)).
- **Three immediate management experiments are offered without outcome evidence**: Rogala proposes cutting two priorities, restoring one-on-ones, and giving the team a buffer week in which members choose their work. No implementation details, baseline, duration, or success measures are provided ([00:18:29–00:19:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iJGKH45w4Q8&t=1109s)).

### Evidence Boundaries

- **The cited figures are prompts for investigation, not evidence about this audience**: The recording reports global employee and manager burnout rates, manager influence on engagement, healthcare work-redesign findings, and Gallup's estimate for the cost of low engagement. It does not identify samples or methods in the talk, and the low-engagement estimate does not isolate burnout or software teams ([00:01:49–00:03:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iJGKH45w4Q8&t=109s), [00:15:52–00:17:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iJGKH45w4Q8&t=952s)).
- **The AI-adoption evidence is too underspecified to generalize**: Rogala mentions an ICSE study in which more than 20% of developers reportedly received no meaningful adoption support, but does not name the paper, sample, support measure, tools, or outcomes. Her separate statement that heavier AI reliance reduces personal agency is also uncited in the recording ([00:05:04–00:05:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iJGKH45w4Q8&t=304s), [00:11:22–00:12:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iJGKH45w4Q8&t=682s)).
- **Personal reset advice is generic wellness guidance**: Doing a little less, slowing down, stretching, breathing, and reducing cognitive input may be prompts for personal reflection, but the talk provides no clinical boundary, comparison, or measured effect. These suggestions should not substitute for professional care or structural workload changes ([00:22:44–00:25:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iJGKH45w4Q8&t=1364s)).
