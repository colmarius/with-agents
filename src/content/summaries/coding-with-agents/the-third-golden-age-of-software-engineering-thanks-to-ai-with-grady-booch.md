Gergely Orosz interviews Grady Booch about software engineering as systems trade-offs and AI coding agents as another rise in abstraction. Booch welcomes disposable personal software and agent-assisted learning, while arguing that durable systems still require fundamentals, architecture, and judgment across technical, economic, human, safety, and ethical forces ([01:59–04:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OfMAtaocvJw&t=119s), [53:50–56:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OfMAtaocvJw&t=3230s)).

### Key Points Covered

- **Software engineering balances systems forces**: Booch defines the work as finding reasonably optimal rather than perfect solutions across technical, economic, human, ethical, and other constraints; code is only one mechanism ([01:59–04:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OfMAtaocvJw&t=119s)).
- **Abstraction rises recur**: He traces shifts from algorithms and data to objects, libraries, services, and platforms, with each layer enabling larger systems while making some prior skills less central ([32:38–38:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OfMAtaocvJw&t=1958s), [46:46–48:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OfMAtaocvJw&t=2806s)).
- **Agents continue rather than begin the third age**: In Booch's framing, AI assistance accelerates use of an already large ecosystem of packages and system components that emerged around the millennium ([46:46–48:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OfMAtaocvJw&t=2806s)).
- **Disposable and durable software need different rigor**: He celebrates single-user, throwaway automation, but conditions reduced concern about internals on the software not needing to endure ([53:50–56:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OfMAtaocvJw&t=3230s)).
- **Agents reproduce recurring patterns well**: Booch says familiar web and CRUD structures fit agent training better than the full breadth of computing; the episode offers examples rather than comparative evaluation ([1:00:35–1:02:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OfMAtaocvJw&t=3635s)).
- **Guided generation can accelerate learning**: He uses Cursor to create a minimal D3 example, studies it, and iterates, while stressing that the domain contains many precedents and that his own foundations help him judge the result ([57:49–58:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OfMAtaocvJw&t=3469s), [1:03:41–1:05:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OfMAtaocvJw&t=3821s)).
- **Developers should move toward systems-level skills**: Booch expects automation in delivery pipelines and simple apps, but sees continuing demand for people who manage complexity, architecture, scale, and human-technical interactions ([1:06:26–1:09:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OfMAtaocvJw&t=3986s)).
- **Multi-agent coordination has precedents**: He recommends studying *Society of Mind*, blackboards, global workspaces, and subsumption architectures rather than assuming agent orchestration is wholly new; these are directions, not evaluated implementations in the interview ([1:09:05–1:13:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OfMAtaocvJw&t=4145s)).
